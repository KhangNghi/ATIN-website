## 1. What ATIN Is

### 1.1 One-line definition
The AgeTech Investor Network (ATIN) is a curated angel syndication platform developed by AgeTech Capital in collaboration with The AgeTech Collaborative™ by AARP. It connects a network of 300+ angel investors to pre-vetted deal flow in aging and longevity technology, primarily seed stage with select later-stage opportunities, and consolidates angel commitments through SPVs.

### 1.2 Approved positioning language
Use these phrasings. They have been through partner review.

1. "A curated angel syndication platform developed by AgeTech Capital in collaboration with The AgeTech Collaborative™ by AARP."
2. "ATIN surfaces and syndicates investment opportunities in aging and longevity technology to a curated network of angel investors."
3. On SPV mechanics (approved July 2026): "ATIN handles the investment operations and keeps [Company]'s cap table to a single line, no matter how many angels invest."
4. On the AARP relationship: always "collaboration," never "partnership." This is a standing correction from the partner team and applies to every document, slide, email, and web page.
5. On the committee: "Expert Selection Committee." Never "moat" or any other label.
6. On AgeTech Capital's results: do not use "track record" framing in ATIN materials. It invites return questions the platform cannot yet answer.

### 1.3 What ATIN is not
1. ATIN is not the Master SPV. AgeTech Capital separately ran a 10-company Master SPV investing in Series A to Series C companies. That vehicle is now closed (as of July 2026). ATIN is the seed-stage platform. Investor communications that go to Master SPV contacts must keep the two distinct (see Section 7.6).
2. ATIN is not a fund. Angels commit deal by deal.
3. ATIN does not independently verify company metrics. All figures in ATIN materials are founder-provided (see Section 12).

### 1.4 Why ATIN exists (origin story, from the sponsorship deck)
AgeTech is a hard sector to invest in as an individual: the buyer landscape (payers, operators, families, government) is fragmented, the clinical and regulatory context is specialized, and quality deal flow is dispersed. ATIN pools sourcing, expert screening, and investment operations so an individual angel can participate in vetted opportunities without building that infrastructure alone.

## 5. The Startup Side

### 5.1 Who ATIN looks for
1. Sector: aging and longevity technology, broadly defined. Pipeline coverage spans digital health, caregiving, smart home, consumer products, financial services and wealth, senior living operations, robotics, brain health, longevity and preventative care.
2. Stage: primarily seed (pre-seed and seed, priced rounds and SAFEs), with select later-stage opportunities.
3. Geography: US, Canada, and Europe (Vermut is Spain-based, Konpanion is UK-based, June Health is Canadian).
4. Signal: founder caliber (repeat founders, clinical and academic leaders), co-investor quality, and directional commercial traction.

### 5.2 Sourcing channels (six buckets, approved August 2026)
1. **Ecosystem collaboration:** The AgeTech Collaborative by AARP.
2. **Partner networks:** the four ACI partners' own networks.
3. **Selection Committee reach:** members sourcing from long-term care, senior living, behavioral science, and founder networks.
4. **In-person presence:** conferences and events across multiple states and provinces (gerontology, AgeTech, investor gatherings).
5. **Digital platform and content:** inbound applications through the ATIN platform, newsletter, and content.
6. **Referral and network effects:** founder and angel referrals from a network that has already transacted.

Accelerator and university channels (Techstars, Aging2.0, Wharton, INSEAD, Cornell, Waterloo) were discussed on the July 8 call primarily as angel recruitment routes, not startup sourcing. Do not list them as startup sourcing without confirmation. **[VERIFY]**

### 5.4 Screening process (what happens between 1,000 and 9)
1. Startup applies (via platform, referral, Collaborative, or partner intro).
2. AgeTech Capital team conducts initial light filtering.
3. Preliminary investment memo on companies that advance.
4. Selection Committee reviews and scores against the 13-criteria rubric. Up to three companies selected per batch.
5. Selected companies go live on Vessel for angel commitments. Standard commitment window is two weeks (extendable if interest continues to come in, as with Jona Health in July 2026).
6. Founder Q&A session held during the window (see Section 8.1).
7. SPV formed for any deal receiving over $100K in commitments.

### 5.5 Materials required from founders
1. Pitch deck (PDF or PPTX).
2. **Intake form** (structured submission). This is the authoritative source when figures conflict. Konpanion did not submit one in July 2026, and its summary carried a visible "intake form pending" flag.
3. Recorded pitch or founder call transcript.
4. Any press releases or memos.

### 5.6 Selection Committee company summaries (established format, July 2026)
Four sections, NDA-facing, all figures in USD where the source provides USD (retain source currency otherwise and say so):
1. Product / Solution
2. Current Stage
3. Traction
4. Fundraising Details

Followed by a separate **source map** (every claim traced to its document) and a **flags section** (material inconsistencies, missing data, diligence concerns). Reference example: June Health. Summaries can be consolidated into one committee document with verification items in an appendix.

### 5.7 Partner review one-pager (established format, July 2026)
Single landscape page, 2x2 grid, one company per cell: what the company does, founder name, revenue and traction metrics, use of funds. An internal second page lists all source conflicts and items needing founder confirmation, and is removed before circulation.

### 5.8 Founder communications

**Register:** warm and inviting, but direct. No over-apologetic or defensive language. Written in Khang's voice and signed Khang.

**5.8.1 "Your deal is live" template (approved May 2026)**
Subject: [Company Name] is now live on ATIN

Structure:
1. Congratulations, [Company] is live on the AgeTech Investor Network platform. Angels have until [DATE] to review and commit.
2. Invitation to forward the blurb below to friends, family, existing angels, or advisors who want to invest but lack a vehicle.
3. Forwardable blurb: intro on ATIN (collaboration between AgeTech Capital and The AgeTech Collaborative by AARP); [Company] is now live; anyone wanting to invest alongside ATIN's angels can join ATIN and submit interest via [unique Vessel deal link].

Each company gets a unique Vessel URL. The two-week window is standard.

**5.8.2 Rejection letters (established July 2026, Nolia example)**
1. Frame around cohort dynamics and batch competitiveness, not company merit.
2. Keep it short: the opening sentences, a note that the batch was especially competitive, a thank-you, signature. No feedback section unless aggregated committee feedback is being shared deliberately.
3. If feedback is shared, it is aggregated across the committee, never attributed, and never includes granular scoring.
4. Never mention that specific feedback is lacking.

**5.8.3 Competitor removal**
If a competitor registers for a founder Q&A, they are removed with a short, direct email (Alita Health precedent, August 2026).

## 7. Investor Communications: The Monthly Cycle

### 7.1 The standard sequence
1. **Deal announcement** (NDA and non-NDA versions) when the batch goes live.
2. **Founder Q&A invitation** ([INVITATION] subject format) and a **reminder** ([REMINDER] subject format) a few days before.
3. **One-week reminder** (both versions).
4. **Three-day (or two-day) reminder** (both versions). Q&A replay offered in the NDA track only, on request.
5. **Last chance** section when a prior deal is nearing close and commitments are already in.
6. **Extension email** if interest continues (Jona, July 2026: "We know this is the summer, and a busy stretch, so you may have missed this one").
7. **Post-close SPV outreach** to specific prospect segments (see 7.6).

### 7.2 Deal blurb format (established standard)
1. Industry label line (example: "Women's Health / FemTech / Employer Benefits"; "AI Health / Microbiome / Longevity").
2. One dense paragraph that leads with the business, not market statistics.
3. Founder credential at the close.
4. Length calibrated against approved references: ManagingLife and Care Daily blurbs for email tone and length; June Health for the industry-line format.

### 7.3 NDA vs non-NDA disclosure split
| Include in NDA only | Keep in both |
|---|---|
| Named clients, franchise partners, distribution partners | Sector and positioning |
| Specific ARR, contract values, valuation, round size, minimum check | Thesis and market logic |
| Named leadership bios not cleared publicly | Directional traction ("doubled quarter over quarter") |
| Q&A recording links and passcodes | Round stage and status |
| Direct "commit" link on Vessel | Founder name and headline credential (when cleared) |
| | Founder Q&A date and registration |

Non-NDA CTA: "For questions about this opportunity, or to learn more about the AgeTech Investor Network, contact khang@agetechcapital.com." Non-NDA one-pager CTA is "learn more," not "learn more and commit." Use qualitative rounding ("a majority of top franchises") rather than approximate numbers, since fuzzed figures can be reverse-engineered.

### 7.4 One-pager format (August 2026, Alita)
Header: FEATURED COMPANY / DEAL ANNOUNCEMENT · [Month Year] / industry label / headline / one-line tagline. Company Fundamentals table (country of operation, core platform, current focus, markets, key milestone). Footer for non-NDA version: **FOR INFORMATIONAL PURPOSES ONLY · COMPANY-PROVIDED INFORMATION**. Do not use a "publicly available materials" disclaimer; content derives from founder-provided materials under NDA.

### 7.5 Subject line conventions
1. Announcement: "New opportunity: [Company] | The AgeTech Investor Network" or "New ATIN opportunity: [Company] ([short descriptor])"
2. Reminder: "One Week Left: This Month's ATIN Deals Close [Date]"
3. Q&A: "[INVITATION] Join the AgeTech Investor Network for a Q&A with [Company], [Date]" and "[REMINDER] ..." for the follow-up
4. Extension: "[Company]: commitment window extended to [date]"
5. Multi-SPV outreach: "New SPVs: [Company A] & [Company B] (closing [date])"

### 7.6 Master SPV prospect track
A third audience: contacts who previously received or passed on the Master SPV. Approved opening (July 2026): "We previously reached out to you about our Master SPV, AgeTech Capital's 10-company SPV that invests in late-stage companies. That has now closed. Separately, we also run a seed stage platform: The AgeTech Investor Network, and this note is about [a new SPV / two new SPVs] that you might be interested in." Master SPV copy is team-approved and not modified.

### 7.7 Tone rules for investor copy
1. Lead with traction and credibility.
2. High-agency, direct language. "We're delighted to invite you" was rejected as too warm for investor copy; the approved reframe leads with the value: "This is your opportunity to engage directly with [Founder], and ask questions ahead of the close."
3. Urgency tied to real timing. "One week left" at one week out; "final days" only at two to three days out. No false scarcity.
4. "Commit by EOD [Day, Date]" for deadlines. Khang sometimes prefers "this Sunday" over the explicit date in follow-ups; do not override.
5. No individual names (committee members, investors) in mass copy unless cleared.
6. "Alongside our angels," not named angels.

1. Every metric traces to a founder-provided document: intake form, deck, transcript, memo, or press release. Independent verification is not required, but traceability is.

## 13. SPV Operations (Sydecar)

### 13.1 Process
1. Soft commitments are collected from investors during the Vessel window.
2. Once total commitments exceed $100K, an SPV is created in Sydecar and each investor's amount is entered.
3. Fees: Sydecar's prorated SPV fee plus AgeTech Capital's management fee (and carry, referenced in investor conversations). Exact fee percentages are not recorded in this handbook. **[VERIFY before publishing any fee figure]**
4. Investors elect one of two fee treatments.

### 13.2 Fee election (approved investor-facing language)
"At that stage, investors choose how to handle fees (Sydecar's SPV fees, prorated, plus our management fee). They can either cover fees on top of their commitment, meaning they transfer more than their stated amount and the full commitment flows into the company, or have fees deducted from their commitment, meaning they transfer their stated amount and the company receives slightly less."

| Election | Amount entered in Sydecar | Investor transfers | Company receives |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fees on top | Stated commitment | Commitment plus fees | Full commitment |
| Fees deducted (net into company) | Grossed-up amount | Stated commitment | Slightly less than commitment |

## 15. Editorial and Brand Rules (non-negotiable)

1. No em dashes and no spaced hyphens anywhere in copy. Use numbered lists, parentheses, colons, or commas.
2. No exclamation points in professional emails or slides.
3. No AI-sounding, generic, or corporate phrasing ("accelerate strategic growth," "high-value relationships"). Every benefit names the specific thing the reader gets.
4. No superlatives or hype. No competitive superlative claims ("no competitor covers the full stack").
5. "Collaboration," never "partnership," for AARP.
6. "Expert Selection Committee," never "moat."
7. No "track record" framing for AgeTech Capital.
8. Concise and direct. Investor blurbs are short and punchy; founder copy is warm and inviting; committee copy is clear and operational.
9. Single price points on slides, not ranges. Cumulative tier structures.
10. Formal but not stiff. No casual openers ("hey"). "Dear Investor" or "Hi [First Name]" for investor emails.
11. Hyperlink company names and founder names (LinkedIn) in emails.
12. Trademark symbol on The AgeTech Collaborative™ by AARP in formal documents.
13. Interpunct (·) is the approved separator in one-pager headers and footers.
